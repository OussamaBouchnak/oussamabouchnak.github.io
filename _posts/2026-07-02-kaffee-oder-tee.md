# Kaffee oder Tee

# 1. Lesson Overview

*   **Communication Goals**: Getting to know people, introducing yourself and others, ordering and paying for drinks in a cafe, stating and understanding numbers from 1 to 1000, and exchanging phone numbers.
*   **Grammar**: The polite request verb *möchten* (would like), present tense conjugation of regular and irregular verbs, asking W-questions (*Wer, Was, Wie*), and the structure of numbers up to 1000.
*   **Vocabulary Themes**: Beverages (coffee, tea, juice, alcohol), cafe interactions (ordering, paying the bill, splitting the check), numbers, currencies (the Euro), and emergency services.
*   **Cultural Notes**: The Eurozone and its currency, standard emergency phone numbers in German-speaking countries (110 for Police, 112 for Fire/Ambulance), and the German lottery system (Lotto 6 aus 49).

# 3. Expressions

*   **Entschuldigung, ist hier noch frei?**
    *   *Translation*: Excuse me, is this seat still free?
    *   *Usage*: Used when approaching a table in a cafe or restaurant to ask if you can sit there.
*   **Was möchten Sie trinken? / Was möchtest du trinken?**
    *   *Translation*: What would you like to drink?
    *   *Usage*: Standard question asked by a waiter, or when offering a drink to a guest.
*   **Ich möchte ... / Ich nehme ...**
    *   *Translation*: I would like ... / I'll take ...
    *   *Usage*: The standard polite way to order food or drinks. *Ich nehme* is slightly more direct but equally common.
*   **Lieber Tee. / Lieber Wasser.**
    *   *Translation*: Rather tea. / Rather water.
    *   *Usage*: Expressing a preference when given a choice (e.g., "Kaffee oder Tee?").
*   **Kaffee mit viel Milch und wenig Zucker.**
    *   *Translation*: Coffee with a lot of milk and little sugar.
    *   *Usage*: Specifying exactly how you want your beverage prepared.
*   **Ohne Milch und Zucker.**
    *   *Translation*: Without milk and sugar.
    *   *Usage*: Ordering a drink black or plain.
*   **Zahlen, bitte! / Ich möchte bitte zahlen!**
    *   *Translation*: Check, please! / I would like to pay, please!
    *   *Usage*: Asking the waiter for the bill.
*   **Zusammen oder getrennt?**
    *   *Translation*: Together or separately?
    *   *Usage*: The waiter asks this to know if they should put all items on one bill or split the check per person.
*   **Das macht ... Euro.**
    *   *Translation*: That makes / comes to ... Euros.
    *   *Usage*: Stating the total price of the order.
*   **Grüß dich.**
    *   *Translation*: Hello / Greetings.
    *   *Usage*: Informal greeting, mostly used in Southern Germany and Austria among friends.

# 4. Grammar

## The Polite Request: "möchten" (would like)

### Explanation
*Möchten* is used to express wishes, desires, or polite requests. It is technically the subjunctive form of *mögen* (to like), but in A1 it is treated as a modal verb. It is softer and more polite than *wollen* (to want).

### Structure
ich möchte
du möchtest
er/sie/es möchte
wir möchten
ihr möchtet
sie/Sie möchten

### Usage
Used universally in German-speaking countries for ordering in restaurants, buying tickets, or asking for things politely.

### Common mistakes
Adding an "-e" to the "ich" form. The "ich" form is simply *ich möchte*, never *ich möchte*.

### Examples
1. Ich **möchte** einen Kaffee, bitte. (I would like a coffee, please.)
2. **Möchtest** du Tee oder Wasser? (Would you like tea or water?)
3. Er **möchte** die Rechnung. (He would like the bill.)
4. Wir **möchten** zahlen. (We would like to pay.)
5. Was **möchten** Sie trinken? (What would you like to drink?)

## Present Tense of Regular Verbs (Review & Expansion)

### Explanation
Most German verbs follow a predictable pattern. You drop the "-en" from the infinitive and add the personal endings. This lesson introduces verbs like *bestellen*, *trinken*, and *nehmen*. Note that *nehmen* is irregular in the present tense (vowel change e -> i).

### Structure
**Regular (trinken):**
ich trink**e** | du trink**st** | er trink**t** | wir trink**en** | ihr trink**t** | sie trink**en**

**Irregular (nehmen):**
ich nehm**e** | du nimm**st** | er nimm**t** | wir nehm**en** | ihr nehm**t** | sie nehm**en**

### Usage
To describe actions happening right now, habits, or facts.

### Common mistakes
Forgetting the vowel change in *nehmen* (saying *du nehmst* instead of *du nimmst*).

### Examples
1. Ich **trinke** gern Eistee. (I like drinking iced tea.)
2. **Nimmst** du Milch? (Are you taking/having milk?)
3. Er **bestellt** zwei Cappuccino. (He is ordering two cappuccinos.)
4. Wir **bezahlen** die Rechnung. (We are paying the bill.)
5. Was **kostet** das? (How much does that cost?)

## Numbers 1 to 1000

### Explanation
German numbers follow a specific logic. Numbers 1-12 are unique. 13-19 end in *-zehn*. Tens (20, 30) end in *-zig*. For numbers 21-99, German says the "ones" digit first, then "and", then the "tens" digit (literally "four-and-twenty"). Hundreds are straightforward combinations.

### Structure
*   24 = vier**und**zwanzig
*   300 = dreihundert
*   150 = einhundertfünfzig
*   2024 = zweitausendvierundzwanzig (Extra example)

### Usage
Giving phone numbers, stating prices, telling age, and playing games like Bingo or Lotto.

### Common mistakes
Translating literally from English and saying the tens digit first (e.g., saying *zwanzigvier* instead of *vierundzwanzig*).

### Examples
1. 21 = ein**und**zwanzig
2. 45 = fünfundvierzig
3. 89 = neun**und**achtzig
4. 100 = einhundert (or simply *hundert*)
5. 250 = zweihundertfünfzig

## Prepositions "mit" and "ohne"

### Explanation
When customizing drinks or food, you use *mit* (with) and *ohne* (without). *Mit* always triggers the Dative case, while *ohne* always triggers the Accusative case. In A1, you mostly see these with uncountable nouns like milk and sugar where articles are omitted.

### Structure
**mit** + [Dative Noun]
**ohne** + [Accusative Noun]

### Usage
Ordering food and beverages exactly to your taste.

### Common mistakes
Using *mit* when you mean without, or forgetting the case rules when articles are introduced later.

### Examples
1. Kaffee **mit** Milch. (Coffee with milk.)
2. Tee **ohne** Zucker. (Tea without sugar.)
3. Wasser **mit** Eis. (Water with ice. - Extra example)
4. Cappuccino **ohne** Milch. (Cappuccino without milk. - Extra example)
5. Eistee **mit** viel Zucker. (Iced tea with a lot of sugar.)

# 11. Common Mistakes

*   **Number Inversion**: Translating 24 as *zwanzigvier*.
    *   *Fix*: Always say the single digit first, then *und*, then the tens digit: *vierundzwanzig*.
*   **"Möchten" Conjugation**: Adding an 'e' to the *ich* form (saying *ich möchte*).
    *   *Fix*: The *ich* form is identical to the *er/sie/es* form. It is simply *ich möchte*.
*   **Asking for Phone Numbers**: Using *Was* instead of *Wie*.
    *   *Fix*: In German, you ask *Wie* (How) is the phone number, not *Was* (What).
*   **Paying the Bill**: Not knowing how to answer *Zusammen oder getrennt?*.
    *   *Fix*: If you are paying for everyone, say *Zusammen*. If everyone pays for themselves, say *Getrennt*.
*   **Vowel Change in "nehmen"**: Saying *du nehmst* or *er nehmt*.
    *   *Fix*: Memorize the irregular present tense forms: *ich nehme, du nimmst, er nimmt*.
