# 1. Lesson Overview

*   **Communication Goals**: Describing how people commute to work, asking for and giving directions inside a building (like a library or office), describing the location of objects on a desk, and talking about dates and birthdays.
*   **Grammar**: Two-way prepositions (Wechselpräpositionen) with the Dative case for static locations (*Wo?*), verbs of position (*stehen, liegen, hängen*), and ordinal numbers (Ordnungszahlen) for dates and floors.
*   **Vocabulary Themes**: Modes of transportation, city infrastructure, building floors and rooms, office supplies and furniture, and cultural highlights of Leipzig.
*   **Cultural Notes**: Commuting habits in Germany, the "Bibliotheca Albertina" in Leipzig, the Leipzig Book Fair, and the city's dual identity as a hub for classical music (Bach, Wagner) and modern industry (Porsche, BMW).

# 3. Expressions

*   **Wie kommen Sie zur Arbeit? / Wie kommst du zum Deutschkurs?**
    *   *Translation*: How do you get to work? / How do you get to the German course?
    *   *Usage*: Asking about someone's mode of transportation.
*   **Ich komme mit dem Bus / mit der U-Bahn / zu Fuß.**
    *   *Translation*: I come by bus / by subway / on foot.
    *   *Usage*: Answering how you commute. Note that "on foot" is *zu Fuß*, not *mit dem Fuß*.
*   **Entschuldigung, wo ist / sind ...?**
    *   *Translation*: Excuse me, where is / are ...?
    *   *Usage*: Standard polite phrase to ask for directions or a specific room.
*   **Entschuldigung, wo finde ich ...?**
    *   *Translation*: Excuse me, where do I find ...?
    *   *Usage*: A slightly more specific way to ask for a person or a facility (like the toilet or administration).
*   **In welcher Etage ist ...?**
    *   *Translation*: On which floor is ...?
    *   *Usage*: Asking for the vertical location of a room in a building.
*   **Im Erdgeschoss / In der ersten Etage.**
    *   *Translation*: On the ground floor / On the first floor.
    *   *Usage*: Giving directions regarding floors. Note: The German "erste Etage" is the US "second floor" (one level above ground).
*   **Wann hast du Geburtstag? / Ich habe am ... Geburtstag.**
    *   *Translation*: When is your birthday? / My birthday is on ...
    *   *Usage*: Asking for and stating birth dates using ordinal numbers.

# 4. Grammar

## Prepositions of Place with Dative (Wo?)

### Explanation
When asking "Where?" (*Wo?*) something is located (static position, no movement from A to B), you use specific prepositions followed by the **Dative** case. 

### Structure
**Masculine (der)** -> **dem** (im = in dem, am = an dem)
**Neuter (das)** -> **dem** (im = in dem, am = an dem)
**Feminine (die)** -> **der**
**Plural (die)** -> **den** (+ **n** at the end of the noun)

### Usage
Describing where objects are in a room, where rooms are in a building, or where you are commuting from/to (static).

### Common mistakes
Using the Accusative case for static locations (e.g., saying *auf den Tisch* instead of *auf dem Tisch*). Forgetting the extra "-n" on plural nouns in the Dative case.

### Examples
1. Der Schlüssel liegt **auf dem** Tisch. (Masculine)
2. Das Bild hängt **an der** Wand. (Feminine)
3. Die Pflanze steht **neben dem** Schrank. (Masculine)
4. Der Papierkorb steht **unter dem** Tisch. (Masculine)
5. Die Bücher stehen **zwischen den** Fenstern. (Plural: *den* + *Fenstern*)

## Verbs of Position

### Explanation
German uses specific verbs to describe *how* an object is positioned, rather than just using a generic "is" (*sein*). 

### Structure
*   **stehen** (to stand): For objects with a base/vertical orientation (tables, cups, shelves).
*   **liegen** (to lie): For flat, horizontal objects (books, papers, pens).
*   **hängen** (to hang): For objects suspended or attached to a wall (pictures, lamps, coats).

### Usage
Essential for accurately describing a workspace or room.

### Common mistakes
Using *sein* for everything (e.g., *Das Buch ist auf dem Tisch*). While understood, native speakers use *liegen*.

### Examples
1. Der Monitor **steht** auf dem Schreibtisch.
2. Die Zeitung **liegt** auf dem Stuhl.
3. Das Bild **hängt** über dem Sofa.
4. Die Kaffeetasse **steht** neben der Tastatur.
5. Der Teppich **liegt** unter dem Tisch.

## Ordinal Numbers (Ordnungszahlen)

### Explanation
Ordinal numbers are used for dates, floors, and sequences. They are formed by adding **-te** to numbers 1-19, and **-ste** to numbers 20 and above. They take adjective endings based on the case.

### Structure
1-3: **erste, zweite, dritte** (irregular)
4-19: vierte, fünfte, sechste, siebte, achte ... neunzehnte
20+: zwanzig**ste**, einundzwanzig**ste**

**Dates**: Use the preposition **am** (an + dem) + ordinal number + **n**.
**Floors**: Use **in der** + ordinal number + **n** Etage.

### Usage
Talking about birthdays, appointments, and navigating multi-story buildings.

### Common mistakes
Forgetting the "-n" at the end of the ordinal number when used with *am* or *in der*. Saying *am fünf Mai* instead of *am fünften Mai*.

### Examples
1. Heute ist der **zweite** (2.) Mai. (Nominative)
2. Ich habe **am** zwei**ten** Mai Geburtstag. (Dative date)
3. Das Büro ist **in der** vier**ten** Etage. (Dative floor)
4. Wir lesen das Buch **im** ers**ten** Kapitel. (Extra example)
5. Sein Geburtstag ist **am** einundzwanzigs**ten** August.

    *   *When is your birthday? — My birthday is on the fifth of November.*

# 9. Things to Memorize

1.  **Dative Endings for Location**: Memorize the rhyme: "Der, Das becomes **DEM**, Die becomes **DER**, and Plural becomes **DEN** + **n**".
2.  **Verbs of Position**: Know when to use *stehen* (3D objects/bases), *liegen* (flat objects), and *hängen* (wall objects).
3.  **Transportation Preposition**: Always use *mit* + Dative for vehicles (*mit dem Bus, mit der Bahn*), but memorize the exception **zu Fuß** (on foot).
4.  **Ordinal Numbers for Dates**: Memorize the formula **am** + [number]**-ten** (e.g., *am vierten*, *am zwanzigsten*). Remember the irregulars: *erste, zweite, dritte, achte* (only one 't').
5.  **Asking for Locations**: Memorize the polite formulas *Entschuldigung, wo ist...?* and *Entschuldigung, wo finde ich...?*

# 11. Common Mistakes

*   **Using Accusative for Static Locations**: Saying *Das Buch liegt auf den Tisch*.
    *   *Fix*: Ask yourself "Wo?" (Where?). "Wo" always triggers the Dative case for these prepositions. *Auf dem Tisch*.
*   **Forgetting the Plural "-n" in Dative**: Saying *zwischen die Fenster* or *zwischen den Fenster*.
    *   *Fix*: The plural Dative article is *den*, AND the noun must add an "-n" at the end if it doesn't already have one. -> *zwischen den Fenster**n***.
*   **Saying "mit der Fuß"**: Translating "by foot" literally.
    *   *Fix*: The fixed expression for walking is **zu Fuß**. No article is used.
*   **Ordinal Number Endings on Dates**: Saying *Ich habe am fünf Mai Geburtstag*.
    *   *Fix*: Dates require the Dative ending "-n" on the ordinal number because of the contraction *am* (an dem). -> *am fünf**ten***.
*   **Missing the 't' in "achte"**: Writing or saying *der achte* with two 't's (like *vier**te***).
    *   *Fix*: 8 is *acht*. When adding *-te*, the 't' merges. It is simply **achte**.
