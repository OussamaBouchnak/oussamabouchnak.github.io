# 1. Lesson Overview

*   **Communication Goals**: Greeting others, introducing yourself and others, asking for and stating names, origins, and current residence, spelling names, and understanding international words.

    *   *Translation*: What is your name? (Formal)
    *   *Usage*: Standard formal greeting to ask someone's name.
*   **Woher kommen Sie?**
    *   *Translation*: Where are you from? (Formal)
    *   *Usage*: Asking for someone's country or city of origin.
*   **Wo wohnen Sie?**
    *   *Translation*: Where do you live? (Formal)
    *   *Usage*: Asking for someone's current city or address of residence.
*   **Was gehört zusammen?**
    *   *Translation*: What belongs together?
    *   *Usage*: Common instruction in textbooks to match items (e.g., photos to words).
*   **Sprechen Sie nach.**
    *   *Translation*: Repeat after me.
    *   *Usage*: Instruction used in audio exercises for pronunciation practice.
*   **Guten Tag! / Hallo!**
    *   *Translation*: Good day! / Hello!
    *   *Usage*: Standard greetings. *Guten Tag* is more formal, *Hallo* is universal.
*   **Und Sie?**
    *   *Translation*: And you? (Formal)
    *   *Usage*: Bouncing a question back to your conversation partner.
*   **Ich hätte gern...**
    *   *Translation*: I would like to have...
    *   *Usage*: Polite way to order or request something (Extra example, foundational for A1).
*   **Das macht nichts.**
    *   *Translation*: That doesn't matter / No problem.
    *   *Usage*: Responding to an apology (Extra example).

# 4. Grammar

## W-Questions (W-Fragen)

### Explanation
Questions that begin with a "W-word" (question word) are used to ask for specific information. The conjugated verb always stays in the second position.

### Structure
[W-Word] + [Verb] + [Subject] + [Rest of Sentence]?

### Usage
Used in everyday conversations to gather details about a person, place, or thing.

### Common mistakes
Forgetting to put the verb in the second position (e.g., saying *Woher Sie kommen?* instead of *Woher kommen Sie?*).

### Examples
1. **Wie** heiß**en** Sie? (What is your name?)
2. **Wo** wohn**en** Sie? (Where do you live?)
3. **Woher** komm**en** Sie? (Where are you from?)
4. **Wer** **ist** das? (Who is that?)
5. **Was** **ist** das? (What is that?)

## Present Tense Verb Conjugation

### Explanation
German verbs change their endings based on the subject pronoun (I, you, he/she/it, etc.). Most verbs follow a regular pattern, but essential verbs like *sein* (to be) and *heißen* (to be called) are irregular.

### Structure
**Regular (e.g., wohnen):**
ich wohn**e** | du wohn**st** | er/sie/es wohn**t** | wir wohn**en** | ihr wohn**t** | sie/Sie wohn**en**

**Irregular (heißen):**
ich heiß**e** | du heiß**t** | er/sie/es heiß**t** | wir heiß**en** | ihr heiß**t** | sie/Sie heiß**en**

**Irregular (sein):**
ich **bin** | du **bist** | er/sie/es **ist** | wir **sind** | ihr **seid** | sie/Sie **sind**

### Usage
Used to describe current states, facts, habits, and actions happening right now.

### Common mistakes
Confusing the formal *Sie* (always capitalized, takes *-en* ending) with the plural *sie* (they, takes *-en* ending) or feminine *sie* (she, takes *-t* ending).

### Examples
1. Ich heiß**e** Markus.
2. Er wohn**t** in Frankfurt.
3. Wir komm**en** aus Brasilien.
4. Sie (formal) wohn**en** in Steinbach.
5. Ich **bin** Student.

## Prepositions of Origin and Location (aus vs. in)

### Explanation
To state where you are from, use *aus*. To state where you currently live, use *in*. Both prepositions require the Dative case when indicating location/origin.

### Structure
[kommen] + **aus** + [Country/City]
[wohnen/leben] + **in** + [City/Country]

### Usage
Fundamental for introducing yourself and talking about geography.

### Common mistakes
Using *von* instead of *aus* for countries of origin (e.g., *Ich bin von Deutschland* is incorrect).

### Examples
1. Ich komme **aus** Spanien.
2. Er kommt **aus** der Türkei.
3. Wir wohnen **in** Berlin.
4. Sie lebt **in** Wien.
5. Ich fliege **von** Madrid **nach** Frankfurt. (Extra example: *von...nach* for travel routes).

## Countries with and without Articles

### Explanation
Most countries in German are neuter and are used without an article (e.g., *Deutschland*, *Brasilien*). However, some countries are masculine, feminine, or plural. These **must** be used with their definite article, which affects the preposition.

### Structure
Feminine: **die** Türkei, **die** Schweiz, **die** Slowakei
Plural: **die** USA, **die** Niederlande

When using *aus* or *in*, the feminine article *die* changes to the Dative *der*.

### Usage
Necessary for grammatical correctness when speaking about specific countries.

### Common mistakes
Omitting the article and the Dative change (e.g., saying *aus Schweiz* instead of *aus der Schweiz*).

### Examples
1. Magda kommt **aus** Polen. (No article)
2. Andrea ist **aus** **der** Schweiz. (Feminine, Dative)
3. Milena kommt **aus** **der** Slowakei. (Feminine, Dative)
4. Er wohnt **in** **der** Türkei. (Feminine, Dative)
5. Ich fliege in **die** USA. (Extra example: Accusative for direction/movement).

# 5. Sentence Patterns

*   **Ich komme aus [Country].**
    *   *Ich komme aus Kasachstan.* (I come from Kazakhstan.)
    *   *Ich komme aus China.* (I come from China.)
*   **Ich wohne in [City].**
    *   *Ich wohne in Frankfurt.* (I live in Frankfurt.)
    *   *Ich wohne in Bad Homburg.* (I live in Bad Homburg.)
*   **Mein Name ist [Name]. / Ich bin [Name].**
    *   *Mein Name ist Ana Sanchez.* (My name is Ana Sanchez.)
    *   *Ich bin Herr Tang.* (I am Mr. Tang.)
*   **Er/Sie kommt aus [Country] und wohnt in [City].**
    *   *Sie kommt aus Russland und wohnt in Steinbach.* (She comes from Russia and lives in Steinbach.)
*   **Ich spreche [Language] und ein bisschen [Language].**
    *   *Ich spreche Englisch und ein bisschen Polnisch.* (I speak English and a little bit of Polish.)
*   **Das ist in [Region/Country].**
    *   *Das ist in Thüringen.* (That is in Thuringia.)

    *   Z = tsett (like 'ts')

# 11. Common Mistakes

*   **Confusing *Wo* and *Woher***: *Wo* asks for a current location (Where are you?). *Woher* asks for an origin (Where did you come from?).
    *   *Fix*: Always associate *Woher* with movement/origin (*kommen*) and *Wo* with static location (*wohnen/sein*).
*   **Omitting Articles for Feminine Countries**: Saying *Ich komme aus Schweiz*.
    *   *Fix*: Memorize the "Big Three" feminine countries in A1: *die Türkei, die Schweiz, die Slowakei*. Always use *aus der* or *in der*.
*   **Pronunciation of the Alphabet**: Pronouncing 'W' like English 'W', or 'V' like English 'V'.
    *   *Fix*: Remember W = V sound, V = F sound, Z = TS sound, J = Y sound.
*   **Formal vs. Informal "You"**: Using *du* or *ihr* with strangers or in official settings (like an airport or course introduction).
    *   *Fix*: Default to *Sie* (capitalized) with adults you do not know well. The verb always ends in *-en* (e.g., *Woher kommen Sie?*).
*   **Using *von* for Countries**: Saying *Ich bin von Deutschland*.
    *   *Fix*: Use *aus* for countries and cities of origin. Use *von* for people or specific buildings/locations (Extra rule).
